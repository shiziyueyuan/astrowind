## Give the investigation a narrative thread

The first PoorerArt ARG episode of the Laplace Puzzle Classroom follows a game and its surrounding online clues. Unusual posts, files, encoded messages and responses inside the game become a sequence viewers can follow.

This case records the video production process within a Blank Puzzle Group collaboration. The game, characters, music and other materials have their own sources; writing, investigation and voice work are collaborative contributions. The focus here is editing and visual explanation, without presenting the team's work as a solo creation.

## Make the materials workable

Gameplay recordings, demonstrations, screenshots, character animation, backgrounds, voice-over and music were organised by purpose and aligned with the script and captions. The narration provides the timeline: a visual appears when the explanation reaches it.

AI tools assisted with organisation, script processing, programmed animation and revisions. Each demonstration still needs checks: does the footage belong to this step, does the text really exist in the source image, and is an interpretation being presented too confidently?

## Explain the method with Remotion

An indoor background and simple paper frames give characters, captions and source material stable places on screen. Fades, small movements and overlapping transitions introduce ordinary material; key explanations advance with short phrases in the narration.

Caesar shifts, ASCII, Base64, Morse and letter rearrangement use different demonstrations. Movement follows the actual method: how letters shift, how numbers regroup and how clues connect, rather than showing only a decoded answer.

## Revise by watching

Revisions addressed repeated text, framing, caption clearance, source proportions, character sharpness and sound. The character returned to its original size to avoid enlarging a low-resolution animation; later versions retained the louder mix and peak protection.

The viewing note was also made specific to the game itself: the game contains mild horror and jump scares, so anyone planning to play should be prepared. Some wording in the existing recording may differ from revised visuals; those limits remain in the production notes.

## Return from the export to the decisions

The website edition comes from the latest viewing-note revision. It preserves about fifteen minutes of the main programme with Chinese narration and captions, in a copy prepared for playback on demand. Character proportions and composition follow the finished cut.

This is Vibe Videoing as a practical workflow: give materials an order, use programmed animation to explain something, then revise through viewing. The record includes the video and the decisions behind selection, pacing and collaborative feedback.
